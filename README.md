# Titanic-Survival-Dashboard
Titanic Survival Dashboard
## 📌 Executive Summary
Cultural narratives often portray the sinking of the RMS Titanic as an unpredictable tragedy dictated by panic and chance. This end-to-end data analytics project debunks that assumption, demonstrating that survival was **systematically determined** by institutional protocol, deck proximity, and socioeconomic privilege[cite: 1, 2]. 


Through interactive Power BI reports and an executive-ready HTML presentation slide deck, the project analyzes **891 passenger records** to expose the sociological sorting mechanisms that transformed an emergency evacuation into an engineered outcome.

## 🔍 Key Findings

1. **Protocol Overrode Chance (The Gender Mandate)**  
   Sex acted as the primary triage filter[cite: 1, 2]. Female passengers achieved a **74.2%** survival rate compared to **18.9%** for males, confirming strict operational execution of the *"women and children first"* mandate[cite: 1, 2].
2. **Socioeconomic Stratification & Deck Elevation**  
   Survival scaled directly with cabin elevation and ticket class[cite: 1, 2]. First-class passengers benefited from unimpeded deck access and proximity to lifeboat stations, while steerage passengers faced physical barriers and evacuation delays[cite: 1, 2].
3. **Compounded Intersectional Extremes**  
   At the intersection of gender protocol and socioeconomic status, survival reached its mathematical extremes[cite: 1, 2]:
   - **1st-Class Females:** **96.8% survival** (near-total protection)[cite: 1, 2].
   - **3rd-Class Males:** **13.5% survival** (lowest across all passenger cohorts)[cite: 1, 2].
4. **Kinship & Resource Pooling**  
   Passengers traveling within family networks experienced significantly higher survival rates (**50.6%** vs. **30.4%** for solo travelers), reflecting organized information sharing and coordinated evacuation efforts[cite: 2].
